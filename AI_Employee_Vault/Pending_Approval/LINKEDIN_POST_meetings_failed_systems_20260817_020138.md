# Every status meeting on your calendar is a system that failed to exist.

Think about what actually happens in most weekly check-ins. People report where things stand. Who's working on what. What's blocked. What changed since last week.

That's not collaboration. That's manual data sync — done live, in sequence, at the most expensive hour of your week.

Here's the test: if a written update could have replaced that meeting, the meeting isn't a meeting. It's a workaround. You're paying humans to do what a pipeline does for free — move information from the place it exists to the place it's needed.

When information lives in a system, the status meeting stops being necessary. A summary is already waiting when you log in. So you meet for the only reason worth meeting: to make decisions, not to exchange updates.

This is exactly what a Digital FTE does on the operations side. It compiles the status while everyone keeps working — invoices chased, leads answered, tasks moved — and hands you the digest. No one sits through a 45-minute sync to learn what could have been five bullet points.

## Key Takeaways
- Recurring status meetings are usually manual sync disguised as collaboration
- If a written update could replace the meeting, you're paying premium rates for data transfer
- Automate the information flow — keep human time for decisions
