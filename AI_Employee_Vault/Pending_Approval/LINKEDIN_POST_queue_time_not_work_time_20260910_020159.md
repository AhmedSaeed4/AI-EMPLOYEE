# That 10-Minute Task Took Nine Days.

Ask why a project ran late and everyone points at the work. The days almost never went to production.

They went to the gaps.

A 10-minute revision that waited 6 days for approval. A brief unread over a weekend. An invoice sent two weeks late because sending it required remembering.

Most project timelines aren't 80% work. They're 80% queue — and working faster doesn't fix waiting.

We treat the gaps as the product:

**1. Map the queues, not the tasks.** Brief → kickoff. Draft → feedback. Approval → delivery. The elapsed time lives there.

**2. Put a watcher on every gap.** An AI employee doesn't do the creative work — it makes sure nothing sits. Nudging the unapproved draft, chasing the unconfirmed brief, invoicing the day a project closes.

**3. Measure elapsed, not effort.** "It only took 20 minutes" is the most expensive sentence in business. The client's clock started when they asked.

Everyone optimizes the work. The margin is in the waiting.

## Key Takeaways
- Timelines are queue time, not work time
- Automate the waiting, not just the work
- Clients feel elapsed time, not your effort
