# Two Vendors. Twice the Meetings. Twice the Bill. 💸

Here's a pattern I see all the time:

A business hires an AI/automation agency to handle their workflows.
Then they hire a *separate* studio for their 3D, reels, and social visuals.

Two contracts. Two onboarding calls. Two sets of feedback rounds. And a silent third cost nobody budgets for: the **coordination tax** — the hours you spend translating between them, chasing timelines, and explaining your brand twice. 🌀

The AI firm doesn't know what the content team needs. The content team can't plug into the automation. So you become the bridge — an unpaid project manager for two vendors who never talk to each other.

The fix isn't a better AI tool or a better studio. It's **one partner who speaks both languages**, so automation and content finally work as one system instead of two jobs you're stuck coordinating.

## Key Takeaways
- Splitting AI + content across vendors creates a hidden coordination tax
- You become the unpaid bridge between teams that don't integrate
- One partner on both sides removes the bottleneck entirely

Your time is the most expensive part of your stack — not the software, not the studio.
