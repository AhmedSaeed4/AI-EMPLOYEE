# Unpopular opinion: You don't need to learn AI. You need to hire one. 🤖

The internet is full of advice telling you to learn prompting, master 12 new tools, and become a "prompt engineer" before your competitors leave you behind.

I think that's backwards for most people. 🙃

Here's why: you didn't become a CFO by learning bookkeeping software. You didn't grow your audience by studying camera sensors. You learned the *judgment* part — the strategy, the taste, the relationships — and you delegated or automated the rest.

AI is the same game. ⚙️

Spending your evenings chasing the next model release, memorizing prompt formulas, and stitching together five apps is just a new, fancy form of busywork. It's keeping you in the weeds when your business actually needs you in the driver's seat.

The move isn't to become an AI expert. It's to put an AI employee on the repetitive 40% of your day — the lead follow-ups, the scheduling, the content scheduling, the inbox triage — so you stay focused on the work only a human (you) can do.

Your edge was never going to be how well you type a prompt. It was always going to be how clearly you can decide what matters.

Stop learning every tool. Start delegating to one. 💡

## Key Takeaways
- 🔁 Chasing every new AI tool is just busywork in a trendy costume
- 🧠 Your real edge is judgment and decisions — not prompting skills
- 🤝 Treat AI like a teammate you delegate to, not a skill you must master
