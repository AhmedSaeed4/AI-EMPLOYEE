# You didn't lose that client to a better offer. You lost them to whoever sent the 6th follow-up.

Here's a stat that should keep every founder up: most deals close between the 5th and 12th touch. Yet nearly half of salespeople give up after one follow-up.

The majority of your revenue is sitting exactly where you stopped showing up.

But here's what nobody admits — this isn't a discipline problem. It's a human one.

Following up *feels* pushy. Awkward. Needy. So smart owners talk themselves out of it. "They saw it." "They'd reply if they wanted it." "I don't want to be annoying."

So you stop at two. And someone less talented than you sends the third, the sixth, the ninth. Quietly, without ego. And collects the cheque.

That's not hustle. That's leverage.

A Digital FTE doesn't feel awkward or take silence personally. It runs your follow-up sequence — warm, spaced, on-brand — until the lead is ready, not until you run out of nerve.

The lead was never cold. You just stopped warming it.

## Key Takeaways
- Most deals close between touch 5 and 12 — yet most people quit after 1–2, leaving revenue on the table
- The follow-up gap isn't laziness, it's ego — humans stop because it *feels* pushy, not because the lead is dead
- A Digital FTE runs the sequence without fatigue — persistence at scale, minus the awkwardness
