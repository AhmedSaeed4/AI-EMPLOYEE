# "We're Not Big Enough for Animation." 🎬

We hear a version of this in almost every first conversation. A founder loves the idea of motion content — then apologizes for asking about it, like 3D is something only big brands are allowed to buy.

Here's the honest answer to the three things that almost stop them 👇

1️⃣ **"It's too expensive."** One animation isn't one post. It's your website hero, your ad creative, your pitch-deck opener, and a month of social clips — all cut from the same build. You're paying for the asset, not the post.

2️⃣ **"It takes too long."** Some projects take 30 days. Some take 24 hours. Scope decides the timeline, not a template — and we'll tell you which one yours is *before* you commit. Honest timelines beat impressive ones.

3️⃣ **"Doesn't AI just make this for free now?"** AI compresses the pipeline — that's why custom motion is finally affordable for small teams. But it doesn't choose your shot, your pacing, or what your customer feels at second three. We use it to lower the cost, not to make everything look the same.

The reframe that lands every time: you're not buying a video. You're buying the first thing every future customer sees. 👀

And the businesses that almost talked themselves out of it? They come back asking what else we can animate — and then ask about the AI employees handling their inbox while they post it. 🤖

## Key Takeaways
- "Too expensive" usually means "only used once" — animation is a reusable asset across ads, web, and social
- Timelines (24 hours to 30 days) are set by scope — a good studio tells you which before you commit
- AI makes custom motion affordable; human taste keeps it from looking generic
