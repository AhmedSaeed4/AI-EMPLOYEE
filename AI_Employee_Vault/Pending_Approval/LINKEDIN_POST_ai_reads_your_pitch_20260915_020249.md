# The First Person to Read Your Proposal Isn't a Person

By the time your prospect opens it, an assistant has already summarized it and decided whether it's worth their Tuesday.

We built one. Our AI employee triages our inbox before we do — and the pattern is blunt: vague pitches sink. Specific ones float.

What survives the machine:

→ **Numbers over adjectives.** "Style frames in 48 hours" survives. "Fast, quality work" doesn't.

→ **One claim per paragraph.** A summary keeps what you said once, clearly.

→ **Answers in the artifact.** Follow-ups now go to the assistant first. If timeline, scope, and price aren't in the document, the answer comes back "unclear."

→ **Plain statements of what you do.** Clever copy reads well. Plain copy gets quoted accurately.

The advice isn't new. The penalty is: it used to be a distracted human. Now it's a filter.

Write for the skim. Write for the summary. They happen first.

## Key Takeaways
- Your pitch is summarized and scored by an AI assistant before your prospect ever sees it
- Specifics survive summarization: numbers, one claim per paragraph, answers built into the document
- Vague positioning used to cost you attention — now it costs you the shortlist
