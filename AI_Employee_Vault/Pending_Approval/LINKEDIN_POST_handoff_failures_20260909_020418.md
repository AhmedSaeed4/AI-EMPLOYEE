# Most Projects Don't Fail in Production. They Fail in the Handoff.

The render didn't go wrong. The moment the brief changed hands — that's where the project died.

A handoff is any moment context transfers: client → studio, brief → artist, human → AI. Every one leaks, and nobody notices until week 3, when the draft comes back "wrong" and both sides insist they were clear.

We treat handoffs as engineered interfaces, not conversations:

**1. Written over verbal.** A call without a recap doc is a rumor. If it's not written, it didn't happen.

**2. One owner per handoff.** "The team will handle it" means nobody will. Name who's accountable for what arrives complete.

**3. Confirm understanding, not receipt.** "Got it" isn't understanding. The receiver plays back the goal in their own words before work starts.

Same rule applies to an AI employee: output quality is a direct function of briefing quality. Garbage context in, garbage work out.

Your bottleneck isn't production capacity. It's how much context survives each transfer.
