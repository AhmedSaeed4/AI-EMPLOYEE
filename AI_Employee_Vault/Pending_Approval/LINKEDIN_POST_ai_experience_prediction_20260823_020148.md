# Unpopular Prediction: By 2028, "AI Experience" Will Disappear From Job Postings

Not because it stops mattering — because it'll be assumed, the way email literacy was in 2005.

What replaces it is more interesting. Three shifts I'm already seeing while building Digital FTEs for clients:

**1. Org charts get non-human lines.**
Not "uses AI tools" — actual roles with responsibilities, escalation rules, and performance reviews. An employee you manage, not software you open.

**2. Direction beats execution.**
The valuable question stops being "can you do this?" and becomes "can you specify this clearly enough that something else can?" Documenting process is quietly becoming a leadership skill.

**3. Team size stops predicting output.**
A 4-person company running AI employees overnight will outpace a 40-person one still triaging inboxes at 9am.

The gap isn't opening between companies that use AI and those that don't. It's opening between those who treat it as a tool and those who onboard it like staff.

The second group is smaller today. It won't be for long.

## Key Takeaways
- "AI experience" becomes assumed, not listed — like email in 2005
- Specifying work for AI becomes a core leadership skill
- Small teams with Digital FTEs will outpace larger manual ones

Where does your business draw the line between tool and teammate? Let me know in the comments 👇
