Unpopular opinion: "3D is too expensive" stopped being true about two years ago — and most small businesses haven't noticed.

3D used to mean render farms. You paid for compute whether the result was good or not, so the default became stock photos.

Real-time rendering broke that. What rendered overnight now previews in minutes. You review actual frames on screen instead of imagining them from a storyboard — no more paying for something you can't see until it's too late to change.

The honest version: 3D still isn't free, and complexity still drives timeline. A product loop and a character animation are different projects — anyone who quotes them the same is guessing.

But the math flipped. You're not paying for machines anymore — you're paying for craft, and craft scales down far better than render farms did.

If your product does something a camera can't easily show, that's where 3D earns its keep.

## Key Takeaways
- 3D is now a craft cost, not a compute cost
- Real frames early = no invisible deliverables
- A product loop and a character animation are not the same quote

What's stopped you from using 3D — budget, or not knowing where it fits? Tell me in the comments.
