# Your AI Isn't Underperforming. Your Brief Is. 📋

Most businesses try AI, get a mediocre result, and decide "it's not there yet."

But look at what actually happened: they typed a one-line wish — "write an email to this client" — and got a generic email back. A new hire given that instruction would ask questions. An AI just guesses. Silently. Every single time.

The gap between an AI that disappoints and one that compounds isn't the model. It's the brief.

**Every task we hand ours gets four things:**

1️⃣ **The outcome** — what "done" looks like, in one sentence
2️⃣ **The audience** — who's reading it and what they actually care about
3️⃣ **The constraints** — tone, limits, and what it must NEVER do (this is where most briefs die 💀)
4️⃣ **One example** — something you liked. Show it the bar instead of describing it.

That's 90 seconds of setup that turns a coin flip into a repeatable process.

We run a full AI employee this way — it drafts invoices, sorts routine email, and wrote the draft of this very post. It only works because the rules were written down before it started. 🤖

## Key Takeaways
- Vague in, generic out — the tool usually isn't the bottleneck
- Constraints beat freedom: tell AI what NOT to do
- One good example beats 500 words of instructions
