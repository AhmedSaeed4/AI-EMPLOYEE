# "I Have Nothing to Post" Is Almost Never True 🎥

It usually means: "I didn't write anything down."

Here's the shift that changes everything: stop hunting for ideas at posting time, and start collecting them during the week. Your business already produced them. You just let them scroll past 👇

1️⃣ **The question a client asked.** If one person asked, forty more were wondering and stayed quiet. Answer it publicly once — that post keeps working long after the conversation ends.

2️⃣ **The thing you fixed.** The workaround, the "why is this broken," the Friday-afternoon save. Before/after stories build more trust than any polished ad, because they prove you solve real problems.

3️⃣ **The decision you made.** Why you chose one approach over another. People don't follow businesses for features — they follow the thinking behind them.

4️⃣ **The thing that took way too long.** Every frustrating task is a future post — and often the exact reason someone eventually hires you.

The problem was never creativity. It's capture. 💡

Ideas invented at posting time feel forced. Ideas captured in the moment feel real — because they are.

Try this for one week: end each day by writing ONE line about something that happened in your business. That's it. Seven lines later, you have a week of content no competitor could have written — because it didn't happen to them.

## Key Takeaways
- Client questions are pre-validated content — someone already wanted the answer
- Fixes and decisions show how you think, which is what people actually follow
- Capture during the week beats brainstorming at posting time
- One line a day is a content system, not a diary
