# My AI Employee Asks Me 40 Questions a Month. It's the Best Thing It Does.

People assume automation means handing over the keys. Ours can't send money, publish anything, or reply to a client without my sign-off. So a queue of questions lands on my desk every day.

Those questions are the product.

Every item in that queue is a decision I've been making unconsciously for years — is this invoice right, is this refund fair, does this lead deserve a follow-up. I never wrote it down. I just answered it, forty times a month, from memory.

The queue forces it into writing. Approve once and it becomes a rule: "Always do X when Y." That category never asks me again.

The queue doesn't shrink because the AI got smarter. It shrinks because the business finally wrote down its own judgment — knowledge that lived only in my head, and left every time I took a day off.

## Key Takeaways
- An approval queue is your unwritten rulebook, surfacing itself
- Every approval is a chance to write the rule instead of making the call
- Documented judgment is what lets the business run without you

What decision do you make so often you've stopped noticing it? Name it below — that's the first rule worth writing.
