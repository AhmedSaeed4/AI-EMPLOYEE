# The #1 mistake people make with AI: trying to automate everything at once. 🎯

The moment most people decide to "get into AI," they grab a whiteboard and try to map their ENTIRE business. Every workflow, every handoff, every tool — one giant automated machine.

Three weeks later it's a tangled mess, half of it breaks, nobody trusts it, and AI gets quietly shelved for another six months. 🫠

Here's the approach that actually works: pick ONE task. Make it boring. Make it repeatable. Make it low-stakes. Hand it to an AI employee and watch it for a week. 🗓️

That's it. No flowcharts. No "end-to-end transformation." Just one task running reliably in the background while you decide what to hand off next.

Why this works: trust compounds. Every clean handoff makes the next automation safer, because you learn exactly where AI is reliable and where it still needs a human checkpoint. You're not betting the business — you're running small experiments that stack. 🧱

Think about how you onboard a real hire. You don't hand them the keys on day one. You start them on something contained, see how they handle it, and expand from there. An AI employee is no different. 🔑

The magic isn't in that one task. It's that the time it frees up becomes the runway to build the next one — and the one after that.

## Key Takeaways
- 🎯 Start with one boring, repeatable, low-risk task — never a full workflow
- 🔁 Trust builds with each clean handoff, making every next step safer
- 📈 Automation compounds: each small win buys the bandwidth to build the next
